<p>Bubble turns Android's own <i>Private Space</i> into a "bubble": a separate, hidden profile where the apps you choose run natively, with their own data, <b>without Google services inside</b>, behind a deny-by-default firewall. Your normal phone keeps working exactly as before, banks and Google apps included.</p>

<p><b>What Bubble does</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Creates the bubble: a minimal Private Space with Google Play Services, the Play Store and their plumbing left out from the first boot.</li>
<li>Keeps Google out: a guard disables any Google service that shows up inside.</li>
<li>Shields notifications: revokes the phone's Google notification listeners and assistant so they cannot read the bubble's notifications.</li>
<li>Filters the network: every app inside starts without internet; you allow each one on Wi-Fi, on mobile data, or both.</li>
<li>Adds apps your way: copy apps you already have, install APK files, or use F-Droid inside the bubble.</li>
<li>Locks itself: the bubble closes when the screen turns off, unless one of its apps is playing music.</li>
<li>Meta space (optional): a separate profile for Facebook and Instagram, with Google installed but turned off.</li>
<li>Bubble Apps, icons and a widget: open bubble apps from your home screen.</li>
<li>Bubble photos and files: pick a photo saved in the bubble as your wallpaper (also from Wallpaper &amp; style, with an optional button over Google Photos) or give it to any app that asks for an image, and browse the bubble's own files. Only what you pick leaves the bubble.</li>
<li>Removes everything in one step, leaving the phone as it was.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>How it works</b></p>
<p>Creating a profile without Google, filtering its network and managing its apps need system permissions no ordinary app can get. Bubble borrows them from Android's shell identity, through your phone's own wireless debugging, paired once with a code, with no computer. Day-to-day actions go directly from Bubble to its copy inside each profile.</p>

<p><b>What Bubble does not do</b></p>
<p>Android itself can still know that a private profile exists and which apps are installed in it; it cannot see when you use them, your data, your accounts or your traffic. Apps that require Google's Play Integrity check (many banking apps, some games) will not work inside the bubble.</p>

<p><b>Status: experimental.</b> Built and tested on a Pixel 9a with Android 17 (stock, locked bootloader). Other phones and Android versions are untested.</p>
